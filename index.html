Learn & interpret

What OSINT can tell you

Open-source intelligence uses information available from public sources. It can add context, but it rarely proves who controls a site or whether a message is genuine.

  • Check the source and time. Public databases can lag or disagree.
  • “No detection” is not a guarantee. New, targeted, or unreported threats may not appear.
  • Confirm through a separate trusted route. Use a known official app or contact channel.
  • Respect privacy and permission. Do not investigate private accounts or expose other people’s data.

Scam awareness library

A little knowledge goes a long way.

Search practical, beginner-friendly notes about common scam patterns and account protection.

M-Pesa & mobile money

Fake reversals and payment claims

Do not send money back because of a caller’s instructions or a screenshot. Check your own account balance and transaction history, then contact the provider through its official app or website if something is unclear.

Phishing & suspicious links

Unexpected links and account alerts

A message may copy a real organisation’s name while sending you to a lookalike page. Open the official app or type a known website address yourself instead of following an unexpected link.

Jobs & advance fees

Pay-first job offers

Be cautious when a job offer demands a registration, training, processing, or placement fee before work begins. Verify the organisation and vacancy using contact details you find independently.

Online shops & marketplaces

Pressure to pay outside a platform

Compare the seller’s details, return terms, and independent reviews. Avoid rushed transfers to personal accounts when the marketplace offers safer payment protections.

Investment & impersonation

Guaranteed returns and trusted names

Promises of certain, unusually high returns and pressure to recruit friends deserve careful checking. Confirm a firm or person through the relevant regulator’s official directory before sending funds.

Passwords & account safety

Protect sign-in and recovery details

Use a unique password for each important account and turn on multi-factor authentication where available. Never share a PIN, password, or one-time code with a caller or message sender.

I’ve been scammed

Start with the next safe step.

Act quickly through official channels. Save evidence, and do not pay anyone who promises guaranteed recovery.

Money sent or a suspicious payment

Contact your bank, mobile-money provider, or payment service using its official app, website, or a number printed on your card. Ask them to secure the account and review the transaction. Keep the transaction reference and messages. Recovery is not guaranteed.

PIN, password, or one-time code disclosed

From a trusted device, change the affected password or PIN through the official app or website. Change any reused password, sign out other sessions where available, enable multi-factor authentication, and contact the provider if you cannot access the account.

Clicked a suspicious link

If you only opened it, close the page and do not enter details or install anything. If you entered a password or code, change it through the official service and review active sessions. If you installed a file, avoid signing in on that device until you have checked it with trusted security software or support.

Email or social account taken over

Use the service’s official account-recovery process from a trusted device. Secure the linked email and phone number, revoke unfamiliar sessions or connected apps, and tell contacts to ignore unexpected payment requests from the account.

Identity details may be misused

Write down what was shared and when. Contact affected banks or services through official channels, secure accounts that use those details, and preserve messages and transaction records. Ask the relevant organisation what additional protections it can place on your account.

Preserve evidence and report safely

Keep original messages, sender details, receipts, transaction references, and a short timeline. Do not edit originals or publish unverified accusations. For current reporting guidance, consult the official Kenyan agencies below.

DCI cybercrime investigations ↗Communications Authority cyber security ↗Safaricom M-Pesa fraud guidance ↗

This is general educational guidance, not a promise of recovery or a substitute for advice from your provider or the appropriate authority. Official processes can change; use the agencies’ current instructions.
